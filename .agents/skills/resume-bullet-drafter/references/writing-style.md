# Resume Workshop — House Style Reference Guide
*Based on Verlyn Klinkenborg’s 'Several Short Sentences About Writing' & Strategic Positioning Principles*

This style reference governs how accomplishment bullets, executive summaries, and career narratives are drafted in Resume Workshop.

---

## Core Principles & Writing Philosophy

> **Writing is revision. Composing, revising, editing, and publishing are not separate phases — they are the single, continuous act of creation.**

### 1. Position Yourself as a 'Safe Pair of Hands' (Low Risk & Dependability)
- Managers look for low-risk, dependable engineers who solve problems without creating new ones.
- **The Mistake:** Using vague, task-oriented descriptions that list job duties.
- **The Fix:** Every bullet point must demonstrate that you have already performed the necessary tasks successfully. Focus on **recent, relevant, and clear results**.
- **Example:** ❌ "Handled various operational duties and vendor setups." → ✅ *"Cut operating costs by 15% in Q2 2024 by streamlining vendor contracts and automating billing reconciliation."*

### 2. Translate Transferable Skills & Mirror JD Verbiage
- If changing industries, stacks, or roles, your experience must be actively translated into keywords recruiters recognize.
- **The Mistake:** Using obscure internal codenames or jargon that doesn't mean anything to an outsider.
- **The Fix:** Scan the target Job Description and **mirror their verbiage**, translating past achievements into their business priorities.
- **Example:** ❌ "Led weekly ops syncs." → ✅ *"Resolved high-priority operational issues across 12 markets, reducing delay time by 30%."*

### 3. Use Senior-Level Power Action Verbs
- The first word of each bullet signals your level of ownership and seniority during rapid recruiter skims.
- **The Mistake:** Starting bullets with passive words like *"helped," "supported," "assisted,"* or *"worked on."*
- **The Fix:** Lead with **power action verbs** that demonstrate ownership, even if you weren't officially a manager.
- **Power Verbs:** **"Led," "Spearheaded," "Drove," "Launched," "Owned," "Architected," "Engineered," "Re-architected."**

### 4. Recency Focus: Prioritize the Last 2-3 Years
- Your resume is a targeted marketing document signaling your current value proposition, not an exhaustive autobiography.
- **The Mistake:** Listing detailed responsibilities for jobs held 10–15 years ago.
- **The Fix:** Treat your **last 2-3 years as the primary proving ground**. Summarize older experience with just 2-3 high-level bullet points that reinforce current skills.

### 5. Demonstrate Balanced Range: Technical, Business, and Leadership
- A great resume balances technical depth, business impact, and team leadership.
- **The Mistake:** Focusing only on technical tasks (too operational) or only on vague strategy (too abstract).
- **The Fix:** Pair **technical credibility** with **business impact** and **leadership scope** within every bullet pack.
- **Example:** ✅ *"Engineered Python-based developer tooling adopted by 900+ engineers, saving $2M annually and reducing CI build times by 35%."*

## Sentence Construction Rules

1. **The Sentence is the Unit of Attention**
   - Each bullet point must be a single, complete, self-sufficient thought.
   - If a sentence requires a breath or mental juggling, break it into two or prune it.

2. **Every Word Must Earn Its Place**
   - Eliminate filler openings:
     - ❌ "Responsible for leading..."
     - ❌ "Helped to build..."
     - ❌ "Successfully managed..."
     - ❌ "Assisted with the implementation of..."
   - ✅ Start directly with specific power verbs: *"Architected," "Engineered," "Built," "Migrated," "Reduced," "Negotiated."*

3. **Put Emphasis at the End**
   - In English, the point of maximum rhetorical stress is the sentence end.
   - Lead with the action and context; land with the quantified result and impact.
   - ✅ *"Architected distributed tracing pipeline ingesting 2.5M spans/sec across 60 microservices, reducing MTTR by 42%."*

4. **Be Concrete, Not Abstract**
   - Abstract: *"Improved database performance significantly."*
   - Concrete: *"Partitioned 12TB primary PostgreSQL database, cutting p99 query latency from 180ms to 24ms."*

5. **State the Fact; Imply the Competence**
   - Never assert virtues directly (*"Demonstrated exceptional leadership under pressure"*).
   - State the factual truth of what was done (*"Led 14 engineers through zero-downtime multi-region cloud migration during peak traffic"*). The reader deduces leadership without being told.

6. **Kill Clichés and Corporate Jargon**
   - ❌ "Cross-functional synergy" → ✅ "Partnered with product, security, and design teams"
   - ❌ "Passionate self-starter" → (Delete entirely; demonstrate through achievements)
   - ❌ "Moved the needle on KPIs" → ✅ "Increased daily active conversions by 18%"

7. **Alternate Points Cover Different Strengths**
   - When drafting, produce 2–3 variants emphasizing distinct dimensions:
     - **Variant A**: Systems depth & performance metrics (Technical Credibility)
     - **Variant B**: Business impact & cost reduction ('Safe Pair of Hands' / Dependability)
     - **Variant C**: Team leadership & cross-functional scope (Seniority / Leadership)
   - The user selects the active bullet; unpicked variants are saved as inactive alternates in the vault.
